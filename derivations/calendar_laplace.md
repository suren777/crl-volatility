# Large-L' expansion of R'(L) for rho <= 0 (epic 0016, task 01)

Notation as in `calendar_band.md`: r = |rho|, lambda = L' = L + ln m, mu = sqrt(2 lambda),
c0 = ln(2 lambda / u^2) + s^2/2, v = ln y - ln(2 lambda). Labels: PROVED (argument written
out below), ASYMPTOTIC (formal Laplace/cumulant expansion; correct as an asymptotic statement
but with no bound on the remainder, so it proves no inequality at finite L'), VERIFIED
NUMERICALLY (script below), CONJECTURED (stated, not established).

## Setup (PROVED, algebra checked by `test_calendar_laplace.py`)

With t = ln y and Z = (t - t0)/s, the conditional law nu is proportional to exp(-V), where,
up to a constant, V(v) = (v + c0)^2/(2 s^2) + v/2 + q^2/(2(1 - r^2)) and
q = mu cosh(v/2) + r (v + c0)/s. This uses a + b = mu cosh(v/2) with a = L' e^{-t/2},
b = e^{t/2}/2. Also y = 2 lambda e^v, L'/y = e^{-v}/2 and Z = (v + c0)/s, so the split
G = -(L'/y + r Z/sqrt y)/(2(1 - r^2)) gives

    R' = -(lambda/(1 - r^2)) [ (1/2) Cov_nu(e^v, e^{-v}) + (r/(s mu)) Cov_nu(e^v, (v + c0) e^{-v/2}) ].

The potential's second derivative is V'' = 1/s^2 + (lambda/2) cosh v at r = 0, and the
quadratic part of V is P v^2/2 with P = 1/s^2 + lambda/(2 (1 - r^2)) + O(sqrt(lambda)).

## rho = 0 (ASYMPTOTIC)

Odd cumulants cancel in E e^v E e^{-v}, so E e^v E e^{-v} = exp(kappa2 + kappa4/12 + ...) and
R' = (lambda/2)(exp(kappa2 + kappa4/12 + ...) - 1). With P = 1/s^2 + lambda/2 and the
quartic term g v^4, g = lambda/48, the Gaussian perturbation gives kappa2 = 1/P - 12 g/P^3
+ O(lambda^-3) and kappa4 = -24 g/P^4 + O(lambda^-3) (both coefficients checked with sympy).
Expanding in 1/lambda:

    kappa2 = 2/lambda - 4/(s^2 lambda^2) - 2/lambda^2 + O(lambda^-3),   kappa2^2/2 = 2/lambda^2 + O(lambda^-3),

so the -2/lambda^2 and +2/lambda^2 terms cancel and

    1 - R' = 2/(s^2 lambda) + O(lambda^-2),   c1 = 2/s^2 > 0.

The first-order Laplace value R' ~ lambda/(lambda + 2/s^2) agrees with this to O(lambda^-1).

## rho < 0 (ASYMPTOTIC; for |rho| s sqrt(L') large)

The linear, cubic and quartic terms of V are of orders sqrt(lambda), sqrt(lambda) and lambda
respectively, so the mean of v is O(lambda^-1/2) and the variance is 1/P with P shifted by
the O(sqrt(lambda)) term mu r c0/(4 s (1 - r^2)). Hence the first term of R' equals
1 - r c0/(sqrt 2 s sqrt(lambda)) + O(lambda^-1). The second term, using
Cov(e^v, (v + c0) e^{-v/2}) = (1 - c0/2) kappa2 + O(lambda^-3/2), equals
-(sqrt 2 r/(s sqrt(lambda)))(1 - c0/2) + O(lambda^-1). The c0 terms cancel:

    1 - R' = sqrt(2) r / (s sqrt(lambda)) + O(lambda^-1),   c_{1/2} = sqrt(2) r/s > 0.

The two expansions are not uniform: the rho < 0 term vanishes as r -> 0, and the crossover
is at r of order lambda^-1/2 (CONJECTURED; not analysed here).

## Remainder and the explicit u0 (not established)

Step 4 is NOT done. What is available:
- Brascamp-Lieb (standard theorem, cited): for mu ∝ e^{-V} on the line with V'' > 0,
  Var_mu(f) <= E_mu[f'^2 / V'']. Applied to the tilts e^{theta v - V} (same V''), this gives
  K''(theta) <= E_theta[1/V''] for the cumulant generating function K of v, and then
  E e^v E e^{-v} - 1 <= exp(int_0^1 (1 - theta)(B(theta) + B(-theta)) dtheta) - 1 with
  B(theta) = E_theta[1/V'']. At r = 0 the pointwise bound V'' >= P + lambda v^2/4 and
  1/(P + a) <= 1/P - a/P^2 + a^2/P^3 reduce this to moments of v under the tilts.
- That route reproduces the leading term and the -2/lambda^2 correction, which is exactly the
  term that the bare bound e^{1/P} - 1 (calendar_band.md, Proposition at rho = 0) misses. The
  missing pieces are: (i) an explicit lower bound on E_theta[v^2] of order 2/lambda, which needs
  a lower variance bound for these tilts (for example a Cramer-Rao type bound); (ii) an explicit
  upper bound on E_theta[v^4]; (iii) for rho < 0, the same bounds with the cubic and linear terms
  of order sqrt(lambda) controlled, which is needed to make the O(lambda^-1/2) term sharp.
- Consequently no explicit u0(k, rho) is proved. Stated precisely: the missing inequality is
  E_theta[v^2] >= (2/lambda)(1 - o(1)) uniformly over theta in [-1, 1] together with an explicit
  E_theta[v^4] = O(lambda^-2) bound, and for rho < 0 the same with the O(sqrt(lambda)) terms.
